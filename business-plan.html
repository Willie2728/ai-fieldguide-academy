Executive summary

The problem is not access to AI. It is reliable application.

People can open an AI tool in seconds, yet many lack a safe task-selection method, prompt structure, verification habit, agent boundaries, or evidence that AI improved their work. The academy combines durable principles with guided practice across popular tools.

AudienceAI-curious professionals, career changers, freelancers, founders, and small teams.
Core valuePractical capability, reusable workflows, and on-demand course guidance.
RevenueMonthly and annual subscriptions, team seats, and later cohort services.

Positioning

Guided, practical, honest.

Against free tutorials

A sequenced path, projects, prompt library, progress, verification rubrics, and a course-aware guide.

Against hype courses

No income guarantee. Demand validation, paid pilots, buyer evidence, retention, and clear limitations.

Against tool-only training

Durable methods—context, evaluation, privacy, permissions, and human review—travel across vendors.

Customer segments

Start narrow. Expand after proof.

Primary beachhead: practical professionals

Operations, administration, marketing, customer success, project coordination, independent consulting, and small-business owners who spend time on documents, communication, research, and repeatable workflows.

Buying trigger: “I know AI matters, but I do not know what to trust or how to use it consistently.”

Secondary: small teams

Teams of 5–25 people that need common prompt standards, tool rules, privacy habits, manager visibility, and an evidence-based workflow capstone.

Buying trigger: “People are already using AI, but our practices are inconsistent and invisible.”

Program architecture

Four layers of value.

Learn

Ten modules, forty-plus lessons, tool demonstrations, and visual mental models.

Apply

Real tasks, twenty reusable prompts, templates, labs, and one capstone.

Guide

Willie explains course material, adapts examples, and helps the learner choose a next step.

Prove

Before-and-after evidence, disclosure of limitations, and a portfolio-ready workflow case study.

Acquisition funnel

Teach something useful before asking for the sale.

Attention

Publish practical short content: one workplace task, one P.R.O. prompt, one visible before-and-after, one verification reminder. Use search, social video, email partnerships, and team-manager content.

Lead

Offer the AI Workday Starter Kit, Paid-Pilot Brief, or 30-Day Team Rollout Map. Capture explicit email consent and source attribution.

Nurture

Five messages: quick win, common mistake, P.R.O. method, Willie demonstration, honest offer. Segment professionals, builders, and teams.

Conversion

Direct prospects to the matching landing page, curriculum proof, product walkthrough, pricing, refund terms, and checkout.

Activation and retention

Onboard into the first completed task within 24 hours; prompt a seven-day win; track module completion, Willie usefulness, capstone progress, renewal, and churn.

Pricing hypothesis

Four levels, one clear upgrade path.

Before launch, validate willingness to pay with interviews and at least five paid pilots. Do not build live clinics, certificates, or team administration as promises before the operating capability exists.

90-day launch plan

Validate the learning and revenue loops in sequence.

Days 1–14 · Customer proof

Interview 15 target learners and five managers. Test the name, top ten reasons, course outline, price range, preferred outcomes, objections, and current alternatives.

Days 15–30 · Paid pilot

Enroll 10–20 founding learners in a guided pilot. Deliver modules 1, 2, 5, 6, and one capstone. Measure activation, lesson quality, support load, and renewal intent.

Days 31–60 · Productize

Revise lessons, record final training, add authentication and billing, connect email, add analytics, test Willie costs and safety, and publish complete policies.

Days 61–90 · Controlled launch

Run one content channel, one partnership channel, and one team-sales motion. Review funnel and cohort data weekly. Scale only channels with acceptable payback and retention.

Launch gate

Do not scale until the pilot proves five things.

  1. Learners complete a useful task in the first 24 hours.
  2. At least one course workflow produces measurable improvement.
  3. Willie’s answers are useful, bounded, and affordable at real usage.
  4. Paid learners understand the offer and want to continue.
  5. Support, model, payment, and acquisition costs leave a sustainable margin.
